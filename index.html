Southern California Edison · Jan 2024 – present

Retiring a legacy orchestration layer

A Python pipeline I designed and built now does the onboarding and provisioning work that a legacy orchestration layer used to do, for 4,000+ action requests a month, and it is safe to rerun.

Problem
Every new hire's request for a laptop, software, a phone or a hotspot had to pass through a legacy orchestration layer before it reached the IT service management system. Retiring the layer removed a major platform dependency.
My role
I designed, built and maintain the pipeline and its test suite, wrote the statement of work, and took the design through a formal architecture review.

Approach

  1. PostOne ticket per item requested, recorded before anything else happens.
  2. FetchAsks for the request number of every ticket still waiting.
  3. SyncCopies the finished records into a reporting table.
  • Safe to rerun. A ticket that exists is never posted twice, and anything still waiting is picked up next time.
  • Fail closed. If a ticket is created but cannot be recorded, the run stops, so nothing exists that nothing tracks.
  • No silent answers. An error is never read as no answer, and an empty read never closes anything.
  • Report before repair. A sanity check and the repair tools report first and change data only when switched on.

Result

  • 4,000+action requests a month no longer depend on the legacy layer.
  • 28automated tests, run in the CI/CD pipeline.

  • SOAP and REST APIs

A performance dashboard for the provisioning bots

A Power BI dashboard I built tracks every exception the provisioning bots raise and sorts it by failure reason. It cut issue triage time by 32% and gives leadership a monthly view of how the automation performs.

Problem
The provisioning program runs more than 4,000 action requests and 60,000+ line items a month through automation. Without one view of the bots' exceptions, each failure was triaged on its own, and leadership had no regular read on automation health.
My role
I designed and built the dashboard in Power BI, set up the exception tracking and failure-reason categories behind it, and use it for the monthly performance review with leadership.

Approach

  1. TrackEvery bot run and every exception is recorded in one place.
  2. CategorizeEach exception gets a failure reason, so failures can be counted and compared.
  3. ReportLeadership sees performance each month across the full request population.

Swiggy · Hyderabad · 2018 – 2020

Cloud kitchens where demand had no supply

Mapping competitors' exclusive restaurants against our own orders showed where Swiggy had demand but no supply. Cloud kitchens filled those gaps, and customers who ordered least often ordered more once they could see them.

Problem
Zomato and Uber Eats were handling more orders than Swiggy in Hyderabad. In some areas customers preferred restaurants that only competitors carried.
My role
I drove the pilot end to end as a senior business strategy analyst at the Hyderabad hub, from the competitor and order mapping through the cloud kitchen rollout and the A/B test.

Approach

  1. Map the gapI collected the restaurants exclusive to competitors, plotted them in Google My Maps and laid our own orders over them. Where competitors were strong, we had few orders.
  2. Measure the distanceI geocoded customer addresses and plotted them against the restaurants they ordered from. Many orders travelled a long way.
  3. Fill the gapWe opened cloud kitchens where demand was high and supply was low.
  4. Win the restaurantsWe showed competitor-exclusive restaurants the demand data and our kitchens' order counts, and brought some onto Swiggy through cloud kitchens.
  5. Test itI matched two customer groups on orders per week, orders per weekend and basket size. One group could see the cloud kitchens in the app and the other could not.

  • On Swiggy
  • Cloud kitchen
  • Order
  • Route

A schematic of the method, not real data and not a real map.

Result

Customers who had ordered least often ordered more once they could see the cloud kitchens. Long-distance orders fell, orders rose in the new areas and the ones beside them, and some competitor-exclusive restaurants joined Swiggy. During Ramzan the kitchens carried haleem, the most ordered dish of the month in Hyderabad.

Grocery Outlet · Internship · Jun 2022 – Jan 2023

A performance scorecard for independent operators

Five pillars give every independent operator's store one scorecard, so a store can be compared with its peers and the pillar that needs work can be named.

Problem
Operator performance was judged through manager feedback and separate dashboards, with no single measure to compare one store with its peers.
My role
My final internship project. I analysed the store data in Amazon Redshift and MicroStrategy and designed the pillars and the metrics behind them.

Approach

I grouped the data by department, by state and financial region, and by quarter, split stores by sales-volume band, and looked for correlations between store measures. That produced five pillars.

Sales
  • Unique sale days
  • Average sales per week
  • Comparable sales
Business
  • Ordered margin
  • Net margin
  • Gross margin
Finance
  • Gross profit
  • Total profit
  • Operational costs
NPS
  • Customer engagement
  • NPS
Store
  • Variety
  • Shrink

Result

Presented as my final internship project, with a dashboard for a wider audience in progress. I recommended a time-series metric that combines the five pillars, and a recommendation engine that flags the pillar where a store falls short.

Writing and research

Working paper · Being prepared for ACM Journal of Data and Information Quality

A seeded-defect benchmark for stage-aware data validation

Most validation suites grow one incident at a time, so nobody can say what kind of check each one is or where it should run. The paper sorts checks into eight types and gives each a home at one of four pipeline stages: source extraction, in-flight transformation, staging and load, and BI consumption.

It tests two narrow roles for a language model: proposing candidate checks from schemas and data dictionaries, and classifying existing checks with a confidence score, as a first draft that a person reviews.

On a purpose-built benchmark of 180 seeded defects across 18 mechanisms, optimized check placement detected 81.7% of defects, against 69.4% for staging-only validation, and cut mean defect propagation from 1.40 to 0.60 pipeline stages.
